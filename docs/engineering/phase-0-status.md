## Summary

The monorepo, the data layer and one vertical slice are in place and tested end to end. An
authenticated Admin API request creates a product under row-level security, and the product's event
reaches a worker through the outbox. Staff sign-in and tracing are in. Spike 5 showed that row-level
security and PgBouncer transaction pooling hold up. It fixed what broke behind the pooler, and CI
now runs every database test through PgBouncer. Spike 1 showed that LiquidJS renders a Dawn-class
theme, in English and Urdu, in a few milliseconds a page, within limits of the storefront's own. The storefront
now renders from documents the worker publishes to Valkey as the catalog and stock change, a
fraction of a second after each change, and serves every shop at its own subdomain, and at domains of its own once their DNS points at the platform, in its own theme, or one it is preparing through a preview link, and with its own menus and pages, and a search that finds its products however shoppers spell them, suggesting them as they type. Links to a shop's old addresses follow its redirects, and a shop can close its storefront behind a password until it opens. Its returns, privacy, shipping and terms policies are at Shopify's addresses, linked from its footer, and the Admin API drafts them in English or Urdu from what the shop has set. Shoppers fill carts from product pages, in a drawer that opens over the page, and change them there or on the cart page, in English or Urdu; the core keeps each cart, priced from the catalog whenever it is read, and shops set what delivery costs, which their pages show. Shoppers then check out on one page, on the shop's own address, its policies linked at its foot, placing a cash-on-delivery order that the shop confirms as any other, or, where the shop gives its bank account, one paid by bank transfer, which waits for the money; the order keeps which versions of the shop's policies they agreed to, and where they placed it from. No order collects more cash on delivery than the law allows, whoever places it: above that, checkout takes bank transfer where the shop does. Shops keep cash on delivery at checkout to the orders they trust, by total, city and the customer's refusals, offering transfer instead, and may charge a fee for it, which orders keep apart from delivery. Shops keep discount codes, for a percentage or an amount off or free delivery, which shoppers apply at checkout: the cart keeps the code, and its use is counted with the order, never past its limit or twice by a customer meant to use it once. The catalog has options, bulk variants, images and
collections, and a shop moving from Shopify brings its products in one CSV file, keeping their addresses. Stock is in: locations, levels per variant and location, an append-only ledger, and
reserve, commit and fulfil operations that never sell a unit twice. Orders run end to end: staff and
apps place them, which commits their stock; cash-on-delivery orders are confirmed or cancelled,
and their items change on the confirmation call, the stock and totals following, until they are
packed, an order placed twice joining the other; parcels ship, are delivered, or are refused and checked back in with items restocked or written off;
and orders close when they are paid or back. Confirmed orders wait to be packed, then to be booked,
and staff confirm, cancel, pack or tag up to 250 at once. An owner or manager gives an order to
a member of staff to see through, and each finds their own. The admin's home says what waits:
orders to confirm, review, see paid, pack and book, parcels coming back, and the cash on delivery
still to come, each with how many and how much; and how today has gone, its sales as the sales
report counts them and the parcels delivered and turned back. COD health follows a period's cash-on-delivery orders
through confirmation and delivery, and what their returns cost in couriers' charges, for the shop
and by city, product, source and courier, and
sales reports say what orders came to in Shopify's terms, without the tax but in its own line, day by day, with the products that
sold most. Prices include the shop's sales tax, which each order keeps line by line and a draft says before it is placed. Packing slips and invoices print from the
browser, in English and Urdu, on A4 or thermal paper. Owners and managers record refunds, up to what
was paid. Orders export to CSV for spreadsheets. Orders taken in chats start as drafts at the
prices agreed: staff place them, or send the customer a link where they confirm the order
themselves, agreeing to the shop's policies as at checkout. Any order's customer can get a link too, to confirm or cancel a cash-on-delivery
order, agreeing to the policies as they confirm it, and then to see where it is. Mutations that must not run twice, such as placing an order, take an idempotency key,
so a retry never does the work again. Every order belongs to a
customer, found by mobile number, whose profile shows what their orders add up to and how they
turned out. Orders from numbers on the merchant's blocklist wait for review, and so do
cash-on-delivery orders whose risk score, from transparent rules, reaches the shop's threshold.
Segments filter customers by who they are and what they ordered, in a query language close to
Shopify's, and marketing consent is kept per channel with a ledger of every change. Customers come
in from Shopify or a spreadsheet and go out as CSV. A customer can have several numbers; duplicates
merge, and a customer's data can be erased on request, at once or ten days on unless the shop
cancels it, while the shop keeps its order records. Staff
other than owners and managers see customers' numbers masked; confirmation agents reveal one when
they call, and an audit log records it, with exports, merges and erasures. Environments and IaC wait
on the hosting decision.

## Next steps

1. **Observability, remaining:** Sentry for errors; Grafana dashboards and SLO burn-rate alerts
   (admin GraphQL p95 ≤ 500 ms, outbox lag, parked events, sign-in failures); the production
   collector with tail sampling, set up with the infrastructure.
2. **Environments and IaC:** after the latency bake-off, write Terraform/OpenTofu for one cell
   (managed Postgres, Valkey, Kubernetes), container images and a staging deploy from `main`.
3. **Spike 5 follow-ups:**
   * re-run the benchmark in the target cloud, with PgBouncer beside the API pods;
   * more statements prepared only as traces show their planning, each checked with
     `pnpm bench:db prepared` (ADR-111, ADR-122).
4. **Staff identity, remaining:** email verification, password reset and invitations by email
   once email delivery exists.
5. **Catalog and stock, remaining:** the media worker (fetch, check and resize images into R2,
   with the infrastructure), and low-stock alerts once messaging exists.
6. **Orders, next:** splitting an order, and changing an order's charges (ORD-04); alerts for
   overdue orders, and telling staff an order was given to them or that a comment names them,
   with messaging (COD-05, ORD-10, ORD-02); payment links once gateways exist (PAY-04).
   **Couriers, next:** booking parcels through couriers' APIs (spike 2, SHP-01), then their
   remittances and tracking through them.
   **Customers, later:** customers asking for erasure and their own file themselves, with
   customer accounts (CUS-02), and a message when their erasure is done, with messaging.
7. **Spikes 2–4** (courier adapter SDK, WhatsApp confirmation, checkout sandboxes) build on these
   packages; they need partners' sandboxes (Phase 0's partnerships track).
8. **Checkout, next:** a map pin and address-quality prompts with the page's first script (CHK-02);
   Turnstile at the edge, with the infrastructure (CHK-18); couriers' own area lists, with their APIs, to check and widen the areas
   suggested.
   **Storefront, next:** the editor's screens with the admin app; the edge itself, with
   Cloudflare for SaaS for shops' domains and their certificates, with the infrastructure.
   **Payments, next:** the OTP (CHK-09) with messaging.
   **Tax, later:** the merchant's tax profile and invoice series, with V1 (TAX-02, TAX-05).
