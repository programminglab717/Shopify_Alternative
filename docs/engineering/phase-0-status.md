## Summary

The monorepo, the data layer and one vertical slice are in place and tested end to end. An
authenticated Admin API request creates a product under row-level security, and the product's event
reaches a worker through the outbox. Staff sign-in and tracing are in. Spike 5 showed that row-level
security and PgBouncer transaction pooling hold up. It fixed what broke behind the pooler, and CI
now runs every database test through PgBouncer. Spike 1 showed that LiquidJS renders a Dawn-class
theme, in English and Urdu, in a few milliseconds a page, within limits of the storefront's own. The storefront
now renders from documents the worker publishes to Valkey as the catalog and stock change, a
fraction of a second after each change, and serves every shop at its own subdomain, and at domains of its own once their DNS points at the platform, in its own theme, or one it is preparing through a preview link, and with its own menus, pages and blogs, and a search that finds its products however shoppers spell them, suggesting them as they type. Links to a shop's old addresses follow its redirects, and a shop can close its storefront behind a password until it opens. Its returns, privacy, shipping and terms policies are at Shopify's addresses, linked from its footer, and the Admin API drafts them in English or Urdu from what the shop has set. Its catalog feed, every variant of its products, is at its own address for Google Merchant Center and Meta's catalogs to fetch, so its ads and free listings are made from its products as its storefront shows them; and once it connects its Meta dataset, each order placed through checkout goes to Meta's conversions API as it is placed, confirmed and delivered, so Meta can learn from the orders that turned out, while its pages load Meta's pixel, which tells Meta what shoppers look at, add to their carts and set out to buy, and whose IDs for their browser the order keeps for the server's events. Shoppers fill carts from product pages, in a drawer that opens over the page, and change them there or on the cart page, in English or Urdu; the core keeps each cart, priced from the catalog whenever it is read, and shops set what delivery costs, which their pages show. Shoppers then check out on one page, on the shop's own address, its policies linked at its foot, placing a cash-on-delivery order that the shop confirms as any other, or, where the shop gives its bank account, one paid by bank transfer, which waits for the money; the order keeps which versions of the shop's policies they agreed to, where they placed it from, and the visits that brought them to the shop, the first and the last from elsewhere, with the campaigns their links named. No order collects more cash on delivery than the law allows, whoever places it: above that, checkout takes bank transfer where the shop does. Shops keep cash on delivery at checkout to the orders they trust, by total, city and the customer's refusals, offering transfer instead, and may charge a fee for it, which orders keep apart from delivery. Shops keep discount codes, for a percentage or an amount off or free delivery, which shoppers apply at checkout: the cart keeps the code, and its use is counted with the order, never past its limit or twice by a customer meant to use it once. The catalog has options, bulk variants, images and
collections, and a shop moving from Shopify brings its products in one CSV file, keeping their addresses. Its images, uploaded from a phone or given by URL, are checked and kept clean of where the photo was taken, and served at the sizes and in the formats each browser asks for. Its link page, at `/links`, gives its bios and chats one address: a line about it, its links, and the products it chose, those with nothing to choose a tap from checkout. Stock is in: locations, levels per variant and location, an append-only ledger, and
reserve, commit and fulfil operations that never sell a unit twice. Orders run end to end: staff and
apps place them, which commits their stock; cash-on-delivery orders are confirmed or cancelled,
and their items, delivery charge and discount change on the confirmation call, the stock and
totals following, until they are packed, an order placed twice joining the other and items
waiting for stock sent apart as an order of their own; parcels ship, are delivered, or are refused and checked back in with items restocked or written off, and what customers send back of delivered parcels is checked in the same way, another size sent at once in exchange;
and orders close when they are paid or back. Confirmed orders wait to be packed, then to be booked,
and staff confirm, cancel, pack or tag up to 250 at once. An owner or manager gives an order to
a member of staff to see through, and each finds their own. The admin's home says what waits:
orders to confirm, review, see paid, pack and book, parcels and returns coming back, and the cash on delivery
still to come, each with how many and how much; and how today has gone, its sales as the sales
report counts them and the parcels delivered and turned back. The storefront counts its sessions,
and how many of them added to the cart, reached checkout and placed an order, and who is on it
now. COD health follows a period's cash-on-delivery orders
through confirmation and delivery, and what their returns cost in couriers' charges, for the shop
and by city, product, source and courier, and
sales reports say what orders came to in Shopify's terms, without the tax but in its own line, day by day, with the products that
sold most, and what they made: each line keeps what its goods cost when sold, and the report takes that, couriers' charges and
what was written off from the sales, and adds back what couriers paid of claims. Prices include the shop's sales tax, which each order keeps line by line and a draft says before it is placed. Packing slips and invoices print from the
browser, in English and Urdu, on A4 or thermal paper, a registered shop's invoices naming its NTN and sales tax registration number as sales tax invoices. Owners and managers record refunds, up to what
was paid, and give what was paid online back through the shop's gateway, Safepay a payment whole, or give a refund as store credit, kept for the customer, which pays their later orders, as staff take them or at checkout once the shopper proves their number with a code. Orders export to CSV or Excel, and staff have their exports emailed to them each day, week or month. Orders taken in chats start as drafts at the
prices agreed: staff place them, or send the customer a link where they confirm the order
themselves, agreeing to the shop's policies as at checkout. Any order's customer can get a link too, to confirm or cancel a cash-on-delivery
order, agreeing to the policies as they confirm it, and then to see where it is. Customers hear
of their orders, placed, paid ahead, shipped with their tracking number, delivered or cancelled, on WhatsApp
from Hatti's shared number, or by SMS where the shop saves or WhatsApp cannot deliver, and by
email too where they gave one at checkout; one who says "band karo" hears no more from the shop.
A cash-on-delivery order asks its customer to confirm it there, with buttons that confirm it, cancel it or bring them its page to change the
address, and asks once more, in the shop's calling hours, when they have not answered three hours
on. Packers book orders with the shop's own courier account, PostEx or Leopards, up to 250 in one
call: the worker books each with the courier, ships it as a parcel with the courier's number,
and follows it, marking it delivered or coming back as the courier says. Each booked parcel's
label prints with the courier's number as a barcode and the cash to collect, and the rider signs
the account's load sheet at pickup. An order waiting for its money, a transfer's total or an
advance, offers to take it online on its page, through the shop's own Safepay or JazzCash account: the
gateway's signed return or webhook records it paid once, and the order moves on to packing.
Customers follow each parcel on their order's page, step by step as its courier says, and are told
when it is out for delivery what to keep ready.
Checkout offers paying online too: the order placed waits for its total, and its thank-you page
sends the shopper to the gateway and welcomes them back paid. Shops pay Hatti for a plan in
rupees, Free until the owner chooses another, through Hatti's own Safepay account, and staff and
locations are held to the plan's limits. Their messages are paid from credit in rupees bought the
same way, each at what it costs Hatti and Hatti's fee as it is sent, and the shop hears on
WhatsApp when a variant runs low on stock, and, at Hatti's cost, when its plan's next period is
invoiced, when the plan ends unpaid and when its credit runs low.
Mutations that must not run twice, such as placing an order, take an idempotency key,
so a retry never does the work again. Every order belongs to a
customer, found by mobile number, whose profile shows what their orders add up to and how they
turned out. Orders from numbers on the merchant's blocklist wait for review, and so do
cash-on-delivery orders whose risk score, from transparent rules, reaches the shop's threshold.
Segments filter customers by who they are and what they ordered, in a query language close to
Shopify's, and marketing consent is kept per channel with a ledger of every change, ticked at checkout too. Customers come
in from Shopify or a spreadsheet and go out as CSV. A customer can have several numbers; duplicates
merge, and a customer's data can be erased on request, at once or ten days on unless the shop
cancels it, while the shop keeps its order records. Staff
other than owners and managers see customers' numbers masked; confirmation agents reveal one when
they call, and an audit log records it, with exports, merges and erasures. Hatti's support looks
at a shop only while its owner allows it, reading alone, each look on that log. Merchants open
their account with their mobile number and a code sent to it on WhatsApp or by SMS, or with their
Google account, and hear of each sign-in from a device new to it. Environments and IaC wait
on the hosting decision.
